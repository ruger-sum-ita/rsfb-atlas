The calendar in brief. The fixed epoch is MC 00 · 00 · 00.000, the Airy Mean Time sol containing the northern spring equinox of 28 April 1970. Earlier years are negative. Years, months and sols are measured from zero. For year Y, apply the leap rule to R = Y + 1: divisible by 1600 → long (669 sols); otherwise divisible by 100 → short (668); otherwise odd or divisible by 10 → long; otherwise short. The 1600-year cycle retains its mean of 668.590625 sols. Months 00–22 have 28 sols, indexed 00–27; month 23 has 24 sols (00–23) or 25 (00–24). Sol 00 of each month is Sunday. One sol = 1000 millisols. Write dates as (-)YY · MM · SS(.fff), optionally prefixed by MC: MC 29 · 22 · 18.675 or MC -01 · 23 · 24.999. MES remains a continuous timestamp from the same epoch.
How the conversion is computed

Mars side: R = Y + 1 for any signed year Y. StartMES(Y) = 668Y + F(Y), where F(N) = ⌊(N+1)/2⌋ + ⌊N/10⌋ − ⌊N/100⌋ + ⌊N/1600⌋. A date is MES = StartMES(Y) + 28M + S + fraction. Floor division keeps the cycle continuous through negative years and year zero. No astronomical year table is needed for civil dates.

Earth side: the chosen zone is converted to UTC (with daylight-saving rules), then to Terrestrial Time using the IERS leap-second table (TT − UTC = 69.184 s since 2017; no leap second announced through mid-2027) or, before 1972, the Espenak–Meeus ΔT polynomials. Airy Mean Time follows the Mars Sol Date, MSD = (JDTT − 2405522.0028779) / 1.0274912517, and MES = MSD − 34241. One millisol is 88.775 s; conversions are accurate to well under a second.

The season shown is astronomical information from an embedded almanac (JPL DE440s, SPICE, apparent solar longitude) covering 1851–2149; it describes what happens during the calendar and never alters a civil date.
